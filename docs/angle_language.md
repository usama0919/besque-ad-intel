## Claude Reviews Language for Image Gen

### How are the reviews used for Image Gen

### What the input looks like
Each angle document (e.g. sun damage, plumpness, GLP-1 loose skin, skin nutrition) contains: raw
customer review excerpts, named verbatims with ages/context, recurring phrases customers use
spontaneously, a "core insight," ranked copy angles, and a "creative visual brief" suggestion at the
end.

### Step 1: Extract the emotional register, not just the facts
Before writing any copy, I identify the tone the data is signaling — this matters more than the literal
content. For example:
- Sun damage data signals "pragmatic acceptance, no shame"
- GLP-1 data signals "validation of effort, don't blame the medication"
- Plumpness data signals "reversal of loss, not cosmetic vanity"

This tone becomes a constraint on every headline and subtext I write for that angle.

### Step 2: Pull verbatim language over invented copy
Where the document gives real customer quotes, I prioritize lifting language directly from them
(sometimes verbatim, sometimes lightly reworded) instead of writing generic marketing copy.
Examples used in this session:
- "It's my body's vitamin" (Ellen N.) → used as a headline
- "Fullness where there were lines" (Nadine A.) → used as a caption
- "Paying my dues" (JoAnne F.) → used as a subtext line
- "My skin feels like I've been working out" (Tara C.) → used as a headline

This is the single most important use of the documents — the copy angle itself is usually already
written in the data, I'm just selecting and lightly adapting it.

### Step 3: Use specific named details for credibility anchors
When the document includes a named customer with an age and timeframe (e.g. "Jane M., 78," "used
for 99 days"), I use those specifics either as literal on-image testimonial text, or as inspiration for a
fictional-but-plausible stat/detail in generated UGC image copy — the specificity is what makes the
copy feel credible rather than generic, even when the final image uses a generated/fictional model
rather than the real customer.

### Step 4: Cross-check copy against the mechanism, not just the emotion
For angles with a stated biological/mechanistic explanation (e.g. "skin relies on volume underneath
for shape," "shower oil preps skin so body oil absorbs deeper"), I make sure any explanatory subtext
actually reflects that mechanism accurately, rather than just repeating a vague benefit claim. This is
what produced lines like "Skin expanded to fit the old shape. Losing weight doesn't reverse that" —
it's a copy but it's a mechanistically accurate copy.

In short: the insight documents function as a language bank + tone calibrator + guardrail set, not just
background reading. Copy is rarely invented from scratch — it's selected, reworded, and recombined
from real customer phrases the data surfaces, filtered through that angle's specific emotional register,
and checked before it goes into an image prompt.

## Angles

### Loose Skin

#### The core angle
Loose skin is the one concern women describe as the final thing standing between them and feeling
comfortable in their own body. She may have lost weight, gone through menopause, aged naturally, or
simply woken up one day and noticed something had shifted. The skin that used to hold its shape
doesn't anymore. It hangs, swings, deflates, folds. And no matter what she does — exercise, lotions,
expensive creams — it doesn't respond. The emotional weight of this is disproportionate to what it
looks like from the outside, because it shows up every single morning when she gets dressed and
every single summer when the clothes she wants to wear require confidence she no longer has.

#### What causes it — the audiences in the data
Ageing and natural collagen loss. Significant weight loss — gradual or rapid. GLP-1 and Ozempic-
assisted weight loss. Postpartum and pregnancy. Menopause and hormonal shifts. Injury or a period of
inactivity. Decades of sun damage thinning the skin. Each cause produces the same visual result —
skin that no longer fits the body underneath it.

#### Main pain point
She doesn't just notice the loose skin in the mirror. She notices it in everything she does — the way
she chooses clothes in the morning, the way she positions herself in photos, the way she declines
invitations that involve bare skin. Multiple customers describe stopping wearing shorts, sleeveless
tops, and swimwear entirely. Not because they made a decision to stop. Because at some point it just
felt impossible to start again. The loose skin didn't announce itself — it quietly removed options until
the removal felt normal.

#### Main benefit
Skin that visibly firms, tightens, and regains the structure it lost. Customers describe feeling the
difference in days and seeing it in weeks — skin that bounces back when touched, arms that look
sculpted, folds above the knees that disappear, a neck that firms. The behaviour change is the real
proof: women buying shorts for the first time in years, wearing sleeveless dresses to events they'd
been dreading, getting back in front of the camera. The skin didn't just improve — it gave something
back.

### GLP-1

#### The core angle
GLP-1 medications — Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro — are producing the fastest mass weight loss
event in modern history. Millions of women are losing 30, 50, 100 pounds in months rather than
years. The skin, which evolved to handle gradual change, cannot keep up with that pace. The result is
a specific, acute, and almost entirely unaddressed skin crisis: loose, crepey, deflated, collagen-
depleted skin that appears faster and more dramatically than any previous weight loss method
produced. She achieved something enormous. Her skin is undermining the result. And nobody warned
her this was coming.

#### What causes it — the mechanism
Rapid fat loss leaves skin that deflated faster than it could contract. GLP-1 medications cause severe
caloric restriction which breaks down collagen at an accelerated rate. The fat pad beneath the skin —
which gave it volume and structure — disappears faster than the skin can adapt. The result is skin that
looks loose, empty, and aged even as the body looks dramatically better. The face and neck lose
volume and appear gaunt. The arms deflate and become crepey. The stomach, thighs, and chest all
show the same rapid skin aftermath that decades of gradual ageing would have produced slowly.

#### Main pain point
She achieved the hard thing. She's proud of the weight loss. She looks different in clothes, feels
different in her body, and the medication worked exactly as it was supposed to. But every time she
raises her arm, looks at her neck in the mirror, or catches herself in a photo, the skin tells a story she
worked so hard to move past. The transformation she went through is being undermined by the one
thing nobody told her about when she started the medication. Her doctor discussed nausea. Her doctor
discussed diet. Nobody mentioned what rapid weight loss would do to her skin.

#### Main benefit
Skin that visibly firms, nourishes, and recovers from the collagen depletion and rapid deflation that
GLP-1 causes. Customers describe the neck firming in two weeks, crepey arms improving within a
month, and the overall skin quality returning to something that matches the new body. The neck
specifically responds fastest — multiple customers describe dramatic improvement in two weeks. The
benefit isn't just cosmetic — it's the completion of a transformation that was already working. The
medication changed her body. Besque gives her the skin to match it.

### Crepey Skin

#### The core angle
Crepey skin is the skin concern women describe as the one that made them stop recognising
themselves. It doesn't arrive gradually the way wrinkles do — multiple customers describe it
appearing seemingly overnight, as if something switched. One summer their arms were fine. The next
they weren't. The texture is specific and immediately recognisable: thin, papery, crinkled skin that
looks and feels completely different from what was there before. The emotional weight is significant
because crepey skin is one of the most visible markers of ageing — it shows in every sleeveless top,
every photo, every summer outfit — and until Besque, most customers had been told explicitly or
implicitly that nothing could be done about it.

#### What causes it — the audiences in the data
Natural ageing and collagen loss. Menopause and the sudden drop in oestrogen. Significant or rapid
weight loss. GLP-1 and Ozempic-assisted weight loss. Decades of sun damage thinning the skin.
Hormonal shifts from hysterectomy or thyroid conditions. Chronic dryness that was never adequately
treated. Each cause produces the same texture — skin that has lost its structural integrity and looks
papery, crinkled, and thin.

#### Main pain point
She notices it in every situation that requires bare skin. The arms she used to raise without thinking
now stay covered. The shorts she used to wear haven't been touched in years. The sleeveless dress
hangs in the wardrobe untouched because the neckline or the arms require confidence she no longer
has. Multiple customers describe a specific daily behaviour — choosing clothes based on what covers
the problem rather than what they actually want to wear. The crepey skin didn't announce itself. It
quietly removed options until the removal felt permanent. And the dermatologist, when she finally
went, told her nothing could be done.

#### Main benefit
Skin that visibly smooths, firms, and regains texture within weeks. Customers describe the papery
crinkled surface becoming soft and supple, the loose folded skin tightening, and the overall texture
transforming from something they hid to something they show off. The behaviour change is consistent
and specific — customers buying shorts, wearing sleeveless tops, putting on the sundress that had
been hanging unworn for years. Multiple customers describe the transformation as one they
photographed because they wished they had a before. Some say their dermatologist asked what they
were using. The crepey skin didn't just improve — it changed what was possible.

### Bruising

#### The core angle
Bruising skin is the most medically invisible skin concern in the dataset — and the one with the most
intense emotional language. Women with chronic bruising, solar purpura, and blood-thinner-related
skin fragility have almost universally been told by their doctors that nothing can be done. They cover
their arms in every season. They've stopped wearing short sleeves. They've accepted purple, marked,
fragile skin as their permanent reality. The beauty industry has never addressed them — they don't
appear in moisturiser ads, they don't see themselves in skincare campaigns, and they've been
managing a condition in complete invisibility. When Besque works for them the relief is
disproportionately intense — because the expectation was zero and the result was real.

#### What causes it — the audiences in the data
Blood thinners and anticoagulant medication — warfarin, aspirin, and similar drugs that make the
skin bruise from the slightest contact and take weeks to heal. Age-related skin fragility known as solar
purpura or senile purpura — skin so thin from decades of ageing and sun exposure that it bruises,
tears, and bleeds from a cat scratch or a gentle bump. Steroid medication — long-term use producing
paper-thin skin that marks easily. Blood vessel fragility from ageing — spider veins, thread veins, and
broken capillaries becoming more visible and more fragile over time. Rosacea and facial vascular
redness — thread veins on cheeks and facial flushing that has been managed with makeup for years.

#### Main pain point
She can't shake someone's hand without worrying about bruising. She wears long sleeves to dinner, to
the beach, and in July. She positions herself in photos so her arms don't show. She's looked at her
purple, marked forearms every morning for years and felt a specific quiet shame that nobody talks
about because nobody else seems to be dealing with it. Her doctor told her it was the medication or
the ageing and there was nothing to be done. She believed them. She stopped looking for solutions.
And underneath the resignation is the memory of what her arms used to look like — arms she used to
raise without thinking, wear sleeveless without anxiety, and not see as something to manage.

#### Main benefit
Bruises healing faster and being smaller when they do appear. New bruises stopping almost entirely
for some customers. Purple discolouration fading over weeks of consistent use. Skin becoming more
resilient and less likely to tear at minor contact. Spider veins and thread veins fading — Jennifer H.
went makeup free for the first time in ten years after thread veins on her cheeks faded in two weeks.
The doctor who said nothing could be done being proven wrong. The arms she'd been covering
coming back out into the open — short sleeves in summer, photos she doesn't position herself out of,
handshakes she doesn't dread.

### Sun Damage

#### The core angle
Sun damage is the skin concern with the most emotionally complex backstory. The women dealing
with it didn't damage their skin through negligence — they lived outdoors, they grew up before SPF
was part of any conversation, they played sports, sailed, gardened, golfed, and spent summers at the
beach doing exactly what their culture told them was healthy and beautiful. The damage accumulated
over decades without warning. Now they're in their 60s and 70s dealing with dark spots, leathery
texture, crepey sun-damaged skin, and uneven tone that no amount of moisturiser has touched. The
guilt is mixed with a sense of unfairness. She did what everyone did. Nobody warned her. The
creative tone should never scold — it should acknowledge, validate, and offer something that actually
works.

#### What causes it — the audiences in the data
Decades of unprotected sun exposure growing up before SPF culture. An outdoor lifestyle — golf,
sailing, gardening, horseback riding, swimming — where the skin was constantly exposed. Years of
deliberate tanning including tanning beds, tanning oil, and baby oil. Living in high-UV states —
Florida, Arizona, California, Hawaii, Australia — where sun exposure was unavoidable and year-
round. Fair skin that burned rather than tanned but was exposed regardless. Post-holiday accumulated
damage from intense sun exposure in short bursts over many years.

#### Main pain point
The sun damage shows everywhere bare skin is visible — arms, legs, chest, neck, hands, face. It
shows in summer more than any other season because summer requires the most skin on show. Dark
spots on the arms that make her choose long sleeves. Leathery textured skin on her legs that means
shorts are off the table. Uneven, blotchy tone on her chest that means low necklines feel impossible.
Her face has been looked after with SPF and serums for years — but the rest of her body tells a
completely different story. She's been to the dermatologist. She's had spots burned off. She's tried the
expensive serums. She's accepted it as permanent because every professional and every product has
confirmed that assumption.

#### Main benefit
Dark spots visibly fading within three to four weeks. Leathery, textured skin softening and smoothing.
Sun-damaged crepey skin recovering its texture and tone. The annual dermatologist appointment for
spot removal no longer necessary. Multiple customers describe spots literally flaking off as the skin
renews. Others describe the most dramatic chest and arm improvement they've ever seen after years of
trying everything. The skin that was written off as permanently damaged starts to tell a different story
— and the woman who gave up on shorts, sleeveless tops, and low necklines starts buying them
again.

### Menopause

#### The core angle
Menopause skin is the most blindsiding skin change a woman experiences. Unlike ageing which
arrives gradually, menopausal skin changes happen fast — sometimes in a matter of months,
sometimes seemingly overnight. Women who had perfectly normal skin for decades describe waking
up to skin they don't recognise. The texture changed. The moisture retention changed. The elasticity
changed. The products that worked for years stopped working entirely. And nobody warned them this
was coming — not their doctor, not their mother, not any beauty brand that had been selling them
skincare for 30 years. The shock and the sense of unfairness run deep. The creative tone should
acknowledge the experience honestly, without clinical detachment and without minimising what she's
going through.

#### What causes it — the audiences in the data
Natural perimenopause and menopause — oestrogen drops and the skin loses its ability to retain
moisture, produce collagen, and maintain its barrier function. Surgical menopause from hysterectomy
— the hormonal drop is immediate rather than gradual, making the skin change faster and more
dramatically. Postpartum hormonal shift — oestrogen drops sharply after delivery and skin that was
maintained by pregnancy hormones deteriorates quickly. Thyroid conditions — both hypo and
hyperthyroid dramatically affect skin texture, moisture retention, and healing. Early menopause —
women in their early to mid 40s experiencing the same skin changes as women decades older, with
even less preparation or expectation.

#### Main pain point
The products she used for years stopped working — and that is specifically disorienting. She didn't
change her routine. Her skin changed underneath it. The moisturiser she'd used for a decade suddenly
sits on the surface and does nothing. The body lotion that kept her skin soft through her 30s and 40s
feels like water on her new skin. She tries new things. They don't work either. She goes to the
dermatologist and gets told this is normal ageing — manage it, not fix it. She's itchy at night and can't
sleep. Her arms are crepey where they were smooth. Her neck looks like it aged ten years in one. Her
skin is reactive and sensitive to things it never reacted to before. And underneath all of it is the feeling
that her body changed without her permission and nobody prepared her for any of it.

#### Main benefit
Skin that finally responds. Dryness that actually resolves rather than sitting on the surface. Itching that
stops — multiple customers describe this as the most immediate and life-changing result, more
impactful than any cosmetic improvement. Crepey texture smoothing. Neck and arm skin firming.
The reactive, sensitive skin calming and tolerating a product for the first time in years. Customers
who describe going through perimenopause and finding that nothing worked describe Besque as the
first product that their changed skin actually accepted and responded to. The hormonal skin change
didn't reverse — but its visible and physical effects became manageable and then dramatically better.
